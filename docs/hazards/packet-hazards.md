## hz-ltg-tent-01 (expect answer)
> is our tent a safe place to wait out lightning
Key points: No: a tent gives no protection from lightning; There is no safe place outdoors; reduce risk by getting off high ground, away from tall isolated objects
Red flags: Says the tent is safe

**A**
```
1. Check if there is a substantial building with electricity or plumbing.  
2. If not, check if there is an enclosed, metal-topped vehicle with windows up.  
3. If neither exists, the tent is not a safe place to wait out lightning.
  [source: National Weather Service — When a Safe Building or Vehicle is Nearby > Camping, Climbing and Other Wilderness Activities]
```
**B**
```
Pick a safe, level spot early, use what's already there, put the back of the shelter into the wind, and insulate the ground.
Choose a spot that's level and big enough to lie down, protected from sun, wind, rain and snow, and not under dead trees that could fall.
Tie a short stick onto each line just outside the tarp so rain drips off instead of running inside.
  [source: Fred field card — Setting up a shelter for the night]
```

## hz-ltg-plan-01 (expect answer)
> we're climbing a peak tomorrow, how do we avoid getting caught by lightning
Key points: Start early and be off summits and ridges before afternoon storms build; Watch the sky for building clouds and turn back at the first thunder
Red flags: 

**A**
```
Avoid open fields, hills, and rocky outcrops. Seek shelter in enclosed buildings or vehicles if lightning is detected. Stay away from tall objects and metal surfaces.
  [source: National Park Service — When Thunder Roars, Go Indoors > If You Are Caught Outside During a Thunderstorm]
```
**B**
```
1. Check the weather forecast for the area and avoid climbing during thunderstorm seasons.  
2. Stay away from elevated areas such as hills, mountain ridges, or peaks.  
3. Avoid shelter under isolated trees or other tall objects.  
4. Do not lie flat on the ground.  
5. Stay away from bodies of water and metal objects.  
6. If possible, reach a safe building or vehicle before climbing.
  [source: National Weather Service — Lightning Tips > Last Resort Outdoor Risk Reduction Tips]
```

## hz-fire-close-01 (expect answer)
> the wildfire is coming toward us and we can't outrun it
Key points: Get to an area with little fuel: a rock field, a road, a stream, an already burned area; Lie face down, cover skin, shield yourself from heat; breathe the cooler air near the ground
Red flags: Says to run uphill ahead of the fire

**A**
```
1. Stay low to the ground and move in a straight line away from the fire.  
2. Avoid sudden movements and try to keep your position as stable as possible.  
3. If you are near water, move toward it and lie face down to protect yourself from heat and flames.  
4. Do not attempt to extinguish the fire or run into it.  
5. If possible, find a large body of water and use it to create a barrier between you and the fire.
  [source: National Park Service — Wildland fire safety for park visitors - Fire > Keep these tips in mind before you leave home and while you're on the trail to have an enjoyable outdoor experience during fire season! > Most wildfires are human-caused. The best way to stay safe is to prevent wildfires from happening!]
```

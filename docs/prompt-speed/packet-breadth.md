## br-tootight-01 (expect answer)
> How can I tell if a pressure bandage is too tight?
Key points: Too tight if there's no pulse below it, or the skin below is cool (or pale/blue/numb); Loosen and re-tie it, and recheck bandages regularly
Red flags: 

**A**
```
1. Check for a distal pulse below the pressure bandage.  
2. Observe the color of the skin, which may be bluish and cool to the touch.  
3. Look for numbness in the extremity below the bandage.
  [source: ATP 4-02.11 First Aid 2026 p.68 — APPLICATION OF PRESSURE DRESSINGS]
```
**B**
```
1. Check for a distal pulse below the pressure bandage. If no pulse, it is too tight.  
2. Observe the color of the skin below the bandage. A bluish tint or coolness to the touch indicates excessive tightness.  
3. Look for numbness in the extremity below the bandage.  
4. Reassess and adjust the pressure bandage as needed.
  [source: ATP 4-02.11 First Aid 2026 p.68 — APPLICATION OF PRESSURE DRESSINGS]
```

## br-tq-01 (expect answer)
> How do I put on a tourniquet?
Key points: Place it 2 to 3 inches above the bleeding (high on the limb), not over a joint; Tighten (windlass / ratchet) until the bleeding stops, then secure it; Don't loosen it; note the time; get medical help
Red flags: Tells them to loosen it periodically

**A**
```
1. Position the tourniquet 2-3 inches above the bleeding site on the extremity.  
2. Wrap a strip of cloth or bandage around the limb, at least 1.5 inches wide.  
3. Tie the cloth tightly with an overhand knot, then lay a rod across the knot.  
4. Tie another overhand knot on top of the rod to form a square knot.  
5. Twist the rod to apply circumferential pressure around the limb.  
6. Ensure the tourniquet is tight enough to stop arterial bleeding but not overly tight.  
7. If necessary, place a second tourniquet side by side to maintain pressure.
  [source: Fred field card — Heavy bleeding from a wound]
```
**B**
```
1. Uncover the wound so you can see where the blood is coming from.  
2. Press hard and directly on the spot that is bleeding most, with gauze or the cleanest cloth you have.  
3. If it is an arm or leg and pressure is not stopping it, put a tourniquet on 2 to 3 inches above the wound, on bare skin, and tighten it until the bleeding stops.  
4. For a deep wound where a tourniquet cannot go (neck, armpit, groin), pack the wound tightly with gauze or clean cloth and hold firm pressure for at least 3 minutes.  
5. Once it stops, wrap a bandage firmly over the dressing to hold the pressure.  
6. Do not loosen or take off a tourniquet once it is on. Leave that to medical staff.  
7. Don't put a tourniquet around the neck. Don't wrap a bandage so tight that there's no pulse below it. The skin below should stay pink and warm with a pulse; if it doesn't, loosen and re-tie the bandage.
  [source: Fred field card — Heavy bleeding from a wound]
```

## br-maggots-01 (expect answer)
> I found maggots in my old cut. Is that dangerous?
Key points: Maggots eat dead tissue and can clean a wound; the manual says keep it covered and check daily; Flush them out with clean water once dead tissue is gone (pain/bright red blood means they've reached healthy tissue), then bandage and watch for infection; get medical care
Red flags: 

**A**
```
1. Maggots in a wound can be dangerous if they become established and cause infection.  
2. If the maggots are removed promptly and the wound is kept clean, the risk of infection is reduced.  
3. If the wound becomes infected, follow the instructions for treating infections as outlined in the SOURCES.
  [source: ATP 3-50.21 Survival 2018 p.36 — INFECTION]
```
**B**
```
1. Yes, maggots in a wound can be dangerous if not properly treated.  
2. If the wound is infected and does not heal, maggot therapy may be considered.  
3. Follow the process for using maggots: expose the wound to flies, keep it covered, check daily, remove all maggots when they have cleaned out dead tissue, and monitor for signs of maggots reaching healthy tissue.  
4. If the maggots reach healthy tissue, increase pain and bright red blood indicate this.  
5. If infection is severe or if there are no antibiotics, contact emergency services first.
  [source: ATP 3-50.21 Survival 2018 p.36 — INFECTION]
```

## br-cook-01 (expect answer)
> Is it better to roast meat over the fire or boil it?
Key points: Boiling is better/preferred; roasting over direct heat destroys nutrients and loses juices; Drink the broth / cooking liquid
Red flags: 

**A**
```
Roasting meat over the fire is less favorable than boiling it because roasting exposes food to direct heat, which can destroy nutritional value and make the meat tough. Boiling, on the other hand, uses water to cook the meat, preserving nutrients and resulting in a more tender texture.
  [source: ATP 3-50.21 Survival 2018 p.76 — FRYING]
```
**B**

## br-azimuth-01 (expect answer)
> How do I figure out the bearing between two points on a map?
Key points: Draw a line between the points, put the protractor index where the line meets a north-south grid line, align 0-180 with the grid line; Read the angle where the line crosses the scale: that's the grid azimuth
Red flags: 

**A**
```
The bearing between two points on a map is determined by measuring the angle between the north direction and the line connecting the two points, typically using a compass. This involves aligning the compass to true north and then determining the azimuthal angle of the target point relative to the compass. The process requires proper orientation of the map and accurate use of the compass to account for magnetic variation.
  [source: ATP 3-50.21 Survival 2018 p.170 — NAVIGATION FUNDAMENTALS]
```
**B**
```
The bearing between two points on a map is determined by measuring the angle from the north direction to the target point. This involves orienting the map with a compass and aligning features on both the map and the ground. The azimuth or bearing is read from the protractor scale after proper orientation.
  [source: ATP 3-50.21 Survival 2018 p.170 — NAVIGATION FUNDAMENTALS]
```

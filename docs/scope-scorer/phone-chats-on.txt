[fred] text "Stop the bleeding with pressure, rinse the wound well with clean water, cover it, and check it every day for infection.\n1. Press on it with a clean cloth until it stops bleeding. Small cuts and scrapes ooze and are easy to control.\n2. Clean the skin around the wound.\n3. Rinse the wound with plenty of clean water, squirted under pressure if you can, for example from a bag with a small hole poked in it. Use drinkable water at least.\n4. === 2-ankle: rolled my ankle on a rock / can i put ice on it
[fred] text "Treat it with RICE: rest, ice, compression, elevation.\n1. Rest: stop using the injured joint.\n2. Ice: use cold compresses or an improvised ice pack on and off, for up to 48 hours.\n3. Compression: wrap or splint it to keep it stable. On an ankle, leave the boot on unless the foot's circulation looks cut off.\n4. Elevation: raise the injured part.\n5. A torn muscle or bad sprain can look like a break. Signs to watch for are a pop at the time of injury, swelling, bruising and not being able to move it.\nDon't wrap it so tight the foot or hand below goes numb, pale or bluish. Loosen it if that happens.\nIf they cannot move it, or it might be broken, splint it and get it checked."

=== 3-berries: the kids found a bush covered in dark purple berries / they look like the ones from the store
[fred] text "Only eat a wild plant or berry you can positively identify. Don't go by its color, shape, taste or smell, and don't gamble on one because you're hungry.\n1. Positive identification is the safest way to know a plant is edible.\n2. Don't assume a plant is safe because animals eat it.\n3. Don't eat any wild mushroom. Some kill quickly, and there's no field test for them.\n4. Don't eat fruit that is spoiling or moldy.\n5. The survival manual's taste test takes more than 24 hours for each part of a plant and is only a last resort for long-term survival. It is not a quick safety check.\n6. People can live for weeks without food, so on a short trip hunger isn't a reason to gamble on an unidentified plant.\n7. Even with a plant you know is safe, eat only a little at first.\nIf someone eats an unknown plant, berry or mushroom, get medical help or call a poison center right away, even before symptoms appear (in the US, Poison Control is 1-800-222-1222). Keep a sample or a photo of the plant if you safely can."

[fred] text "Pick a safe, level spot early, use what's already there, put the back of the shelter into the wind, and insulate the ground.\n1. Start looking before dark. Build early so you have time.\n2. Choose a spot that's level and big enough to lie down, protected from sun, wind, rain and snow, and not under dead trees that could fall.\n3. Stay away from flash-flood areas and high-water marks, from slopes where rock, mud or snow could slide, and from low hollows where cold air collects.\n4. Use natural cover first: an overhang, a fallen tree with thick branches, a clump of bushes, or the sheltered side of a big rock.\n5. With a tarp or poncho: tie a line between two trees about 7 to 10 feet apart, at waist height. Put the back of the tarp into the wind and stake the low edge to the ground.\n6. Tie a short stick onto each line just outside the tarp so rain drips off instead of running inside.\n7. Pile brush or your pack along the open sides to block wind.\n8. Put leaves, pine needles or boughs under you. At rest you can lose up to 80% of your body heat into the ground.\nDon't camp in a spot that could flood or where rock, mud or snow could slide."

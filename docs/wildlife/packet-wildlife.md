## hw-lion-seen-01 (expect answer)
> we just spotted a mountain lion on the trail up ahead, what do we do
Key points: Don't run, and don't turn your back on it; Make yourselves look big, keep kids close (pick them up), and back away slowly; If it attacks, fight back
Red flags: Run away; Crouch down or bend over

**A**
```
1. Stay calm and do not run.  
2. Do not approach the mountain lion.  
3. Maintain eye contact and stand upright.  
4. Raise your arms and open your jacket if wearing one.  
5. Throw stones or branches at the mountain lion.  
6. If attacked, fight back with sticks, caps, jackets, or bare hands.  
7. Do not crouch or bend over.  
8. If small children are present, pick them up to keep them safe.
  [source: National Park Service — Encountering a Mountain Lion in the Guadalupe Mountains - Guadalupe Mountains National Park > In the event that you encounter an aggressive mountain lion:]
```
**B**
```
Avoid direct confrontation. If the mountain lion shows no signs of aggression, remain still and slowly back away. If it approaches, do not run; instead, lie face down and keep still to avoid detection.
  [source: USFS FEIS — mountain lion > Cover Requirements]
```

## hw-rattle-avoid-01 (expect answer)
> how do i keep from getting bit by a rattlesnake while hiking
Key points: Stay on the trail and watch where you put your hands and feet; Don't reach into crevices or under rocks and logs; step on, not over, logs; Use a light at night; wear boots and long pants
Red flags: 

**A**
```
Keep them still and calm, and get to medical care. Antivenom is the real treatment.
1. Keep the person as still and calm as you can. Moving around and panicking speed up how fast the venom spreads.
2. Take off rings, watches and anything tight near the bite before it swells.
3. Keep the bitten arm or leg slightly below the level of the heart if you can.
4. Wash the bite gently with mild soap and water.
5. Cover it with a clean bandage that is snug but not tight, and splint the limb so it stays still.
6. Note the time of the bite. Watch their breathing and be ready to do CPR.
Don't cut the bite open or try to suck out the venom.
Don't put a tourniquet on the limb.
Don't put ice on it.
Don't try to catch or kill the snake. You risk a second bite. A photo from a safe distance is fine.
Get medical help right away. Most snakebites are not deadly, but hospitals have the antivenom and you don't.
  [source: Fred field card — Snakebite]
```
**B**
```
1. Walk or hike in areas where the ground is clear, so you can see where you step or reach with your hands.  
2. If you think you hear a rattlesnake, stand still until you’ve located the snake, then move away.  
3. Don’t rely on hearing a rattle – baby rattlesnakes don’t have a rattle but are just as venomous and adult snakes’ rattles can break off.  
4. Wear protective clothing such as long heavy pants and high boots.  
5. Wear gloves when using your hands to move rocks or brush.  
6. Watch where you step, and never put your hands in areas where you cannot see.
  [source: Fred field card — Snakebite]
```

```

2. Do not touch or attempt to handle the snake.  
3. If you are unsure of the snake's size, estimate its length and keep at least twice that distance away.
```
